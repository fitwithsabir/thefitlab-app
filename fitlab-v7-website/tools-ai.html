How Coach AI works — and what you're responsible for

  • You stay the professional. Coach AI drafts plans for you to review, edit and approve. It doesn't diagnose, treat or prescribe, and it isn't a substitute for a doctor or registered dietitian. Check every plan before you share it.
  • Numbers come from published sources. Calories and macros are worked out from the Indian Food Composition Tables 2017 (NIN-ICMR) and USDA FoodData Central, targets from Mifflin-St Jeor, ISSN, ICMR-NIN and IOM guidance, and blood results are read against ADA 2024, NCEP ATP III, WHO, KDIGO 2024 and other cut-offs listed on each plan.
  • Consent first. Only add a client's health details or blood report with their clear consent, and note how they gave it. They can ask you to delete it at any time — delete removes the file and the values.
  • What the AI sees. To make a plan, Coach AI sends the client's age, sex, measurements, goal, food preference, allergies, health screening and blood-test values to our AI provider (Anthropic) — never their name. To read a report, the file you upload is sent, and it may show the client's name. The provider processes it to give the answer under its commercial terms, which say API data isn't used to train its models.
  • Refer when it says refer. Results flagged "discuss with the doctor" — very high sugar, low haemoglobin, kidney or liver changes — need a medical opinion before you change the plan.
